Drone & Visual Inspection

What can a drone roof inspection identify?

PCT Property Intelligence·Drone & Visual Inspection
What can a drone roof inspection identify?

Roofs are one of the hardest parts of a property to inspect properly. Ground-level viewing only shows you certain angles, and access equipment like ladders or scaffolding can be slow, costly, and not always necessary just to have a look. A drone gives a safe, efficient way to see roof areas clearly — but it's worth understanding what that inspection can and can't tell you.

What a drone roof inspection can identify

  • Visible surface condition — missing, slipped or damaged tiles and slates, visible cracking, and general wear.
  • Moss and debris build-up on roof surfaces and in valleys or gutters.
  • Visible flashing issues around chimneys, joints and roof penetrations.
  • General condition of chimneys and elevated features that are difficult to see from the ground.
  • Clear photographic evidence you can keep on file, refer back to, or share with contractors.

What it doesn't replace

A drone inspection is a visual inspection. It provides condition observations and photographic evidence — it does not replace a structural survey, and it can't identify issues that aren't visible from the outside, such as problems with roof timbers, insulation, or ventilation inside the roof space. Where a finding suggests something more serious, the right next step is a referral to an appropriately qualified specialist.

When it's most useful

  • Before buying a property, as a supplement to (not a replacement for) a formal survey.
  • After a storm, to check for obvious visible damage.
  • As part of routine maintenance, to catch small issues before they become bigger ones.
  • Alongside a Roof Inspection or Gutter Inspection, for a fuller picture in one visit.
